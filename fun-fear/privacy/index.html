Fun Fear Privacy Policy

Effective date: 12 October 2026 (updated the same day: the update check described in full)

Fun Fear ("the game") is a pass-and-play memory board game for iPad, published by PINtPOINT. This policy explains what happens to your information when you play it. In short: nothing about you or your game leaves your iPad.

What we do not collect

  • No account and no sign-up. There is nothing to log in to.
  • No analytics, no tracking, no advertising and no advertising identifiers.
  • No in-app purchases and no subscriptions.
  • The publisher does not collect, receive or store any personal data or usage data from the game. The one exception to the game talking to nobody is the update check described below, which goes to Expo rather than to us.

What stays on your iPad

The game in progress, the names and counters players choose, the difficulty setting and the sound and music switches are stored only on your iPad. They are not sent anywhere. They are removed when you delete the game.

Player names are whatever people type. The game does not ask for a real name and never sends one anywhere, so if a child types their own name it stays on the device exactly like the rest of the saved game.

What the game needs

Fun Fear does not ask for your location, your camera, your microphone, your contacts or your photos. It plays entirely on one device, passed round the table.

The one connection it makes

The game asks whether a newer version of itself is available, and downloads it if there is one. That request goes to Expo's update service, which is run by Expo (650 Industries, Inc.) and not by us. Expo's privacy policy applies to what it receives.

The request carries:

  • the platform (iOS) and the version of the game on your device;
  • the identifier of the update already installed;
  • a randomly generated installation identifier, which the updates library creates the first time the game runs and keeps on the device until the game is deleted. It is not your name, your Apple ID or an advertising identifier, it is not linked to any account, and it is not used for advertising or tracking across apps or websites;
  • if the game failed to start the last time it was opened, the error message, so that a working version can be sent instead.

As with any request to any website, Expo's servers also receive your device's IP address and ordinary network and operating-system information. Expo may keep that for the running and security of the service.

The request carries nothing about the game you are playing: no player names, no scores, no settings, no record of who won.

Everything else in the game — the board, the artwork, the cards, the opponents — is on the device already. Fun Fear is completely playable with no internet connection at all; the update check is the only connection it ever makes.

Children

Fun Fear is a board game, and children are expected to play it. There is no chat, no messaging, no user accounts, no links out of the game to the web, no advertising, and nothing to buy. The publisher receives nothing about any player, child or adult.

The update check described above is the only third party the game ever contacts, and it is contacted to deliver fixes to the game rather than to learn anything about the person playing. It sends no name, no age and nothing a child types into the game.

Your rights

Because the publisher holds no personal data about you from Fun Fear, there is nothing for us to access, export or delete. Deleting the app removes everything it has stored on your device. For questions, contact hello@pintpoint.co.uk.

This website

pintpoint.co.uk counts page views with cookieless Cloudflare Web Analytics and a record on our own server. Neither sets a cookie or identifies you — see the PINtPOINT website privacy policy. The Fun Fear app itself sends us nothing.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted at this address with a new effective date.

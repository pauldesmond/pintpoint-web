Left Field Beer and the Price of American Craft in Britain

Left Field Beer's range runs from Hop Butcher out of Chicago and Trillium out of Massachusetts, through European specialists like Arpus and CoolHead, to UK independents including DEYA and Buxton.

This piece spends most of its time on the American end of the range, partly because it's where the prices sting hardest, and partly because the structural reasons behind those prices are the most useful thing to explain.

Cloudwater's co-founder Paul Jones told Zythophile in 2016 that the brewery had moved towards the style of Vermont beers such as Heady Topper. The actual American product is stubbornly hard to find here.

Why Left Field matters

Left Field Beer Limited was incorporated in January 2016 and is registered in Solihull. Its American breweries include Trillium and Hop Butcher For The World.

The European range includes Arpus from Latvia and Factory and CoolHead from Finland. UK breweries include DEYA from Cheltenham and Buxton from the Peak District.

Left Field runs a monthly beer club of 6 or 12 hand-picked beers, a Build-a-Box service that lets you add to one box with separate orders over two months, pre-orders, and a price match that aims to “match the prices charged directly by UK breweries wherever possible”.

Why the prices sting

Imported American craft is expensive. On 4 November 2026 Left Field listed twenty Trillium beers from £7.00 to £22.00, half of them under £9.

The costs include:

  • Freight. Beer is heavy, and it has to cross the Atlantic.
  • Cold chain. Hazy IPAs are perishable. Heat kills them. Keeping them cold from US brewery floor to your fridge means refrigerated shipping, refrigerated storage, and refrigerated delivery.
  • UK beer duty. Charged by strength. HMRC's rates from 1 February 2026 are £22.58 per litre of pure alcohol for beer from 3.5% to 8.4%, and £30.62 from 8.5%. That works out at about 75p on a 473ml can at 7%, and about £1.23 at 8.5%.
  • VAT. Twenty percent on top of everything above.
  • Customs handling and admin.
  • Tariff disruption. In 2025 the US raised its tariffs on imported aluminium and extended them to empty aluminium cans, which the Brewers Association says “hit brewers particularly hard”.

Important nuance

Not every expensive American beer in the UK is automatically worth it. A £10 can of something past its best, warm-shipped, sitting on a shelf for three months, is not a good deal regardless of which brewery's name is on the side.

What separates a serious importer from a speculative one is the quiet, expensive part: keeping the chain cold, rotating stock, and selling releases before they go stale. Freshness is part of what the price pays for.

Brewed-under-licence American beer is a different proposition entirely. A UK-made version of a legacy American recipe is cheaper because it skipped the ocean and the handling, but it is not the same liquid. Different water, different yeast, different hops. That suits long-established brands. It does not give you the current releases where the point is the specific brewery's specific expression, fresh.

What Left Field is actually selling

If you want current American releases imported rather than brewed under licence, Left Field Beer stocks them.

The same judgment runs through the rest of their range, though the economics differ, since a Buxton or a DEYA carries no transatlantic freight.

Frequently asked questions

Why is American craft beer so expensive in the UK?

Imported American beer carries freight, cold-chain handling, UK beer duty, VAT and customs costs. The 2025 US tariffs on aluminium also push up the cost of the can itself at the American end.

What is Left Field Beer?

Left Field Beer is a UK online retailer specialising in craft beer imports, based in Solihull and trading since 2016. They're an online retailer of fresh American craft, including Trillium, alongside a wider curation of European standouts like Arpus, Factory and CoolHead and UK independents. They run a monthly subscription beer club, a Build-a-Box service, and pre-orders on upcoming releases. Their site is at leftfieldbeer.co.uk.

Can you get Trillium or Tree House in the UK?

Trillium, yes. Left Field Beer regularly stocks fresh Trillium among other high-demand US imports. On 4 November 2026 Left Field's breweries page did not list Tree House. Availability changes week to week, so check the shop directly.

What's the difference between imported and brewed-under-licence US beer?

Imported US beer is physically brewed in the United States and shipped over, the same liquid the brewery's home market drinks. Brewed-under-licence means a UK or European brewery makes a version of the beer from the original recipe, under agreement. The latter is cheaper because it avoids the freight and the cold chain, but it is a different product: different water, different yeast cultures, often slightly different hops. For legacy names brewed-under-licence is widespread, though Sierra Nevada reaches the UK as an import rather than a licensed brew; for hype-driven modern releases, imported is the only real version.

Is a £10 imported can of American craft beer worth it?

An imported fresh-in-date Trillium can under £9 is expensive for real reasons (freight, duty, handling, cold chain) but still represents one of the few routes to drinking a world-class American hazy IPA on this side of the Atlantic. A tired can of the same thing, date-worn and warm-shipped, isn't worth it. A good importer earns the premium by getting it fresh, holding it cold, and charging transparently.

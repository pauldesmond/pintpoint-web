The Unholy Trinity

Three of the autumn’s great nights fall inside a single week: Halloween on the thirty-first, Lewes Bonfire on the fifth, and J-dag, when Tuborg’s Christmas beer goes on sale at 20:59 on the first Friday of November.

In each one something that looks old turns out not to be, and the beer is the evidence. The question is who did the inventing, and how far that was from the people who keep it.

There is an essay on each.

Part one · Monday 26 October

Season of the Witch

A 1939 article in Folklore gave the carved leafy faces in churches a pagan explanation and a name, the Green Man, that was already on pub signs. Also: why colonial Americans brewed with pumpkin when other ingredients were scarce, and why green hop beer follows the picking schedule rather than Halloween.

Part two · Monday 2 November

Voice of the Fire

Lewes burns an effigy of Paul V, a Victorian custom, in a celebration the Bonfire Boys revived from the 1820s. The processions pass Harvey’s brewery, where Bonfire Boy is brewed.

Part three · Friday 6 November · to come

The First Friday of Christmas

A Norwegian king once made brewing Yule ale compulsory. A thousand years later a Danish brewery set a date for the start of Christmas, and later moved it to one minute to nine on a Friday night. In between, an American brewed a Christmas beer in 1975 that, as far as he could discover, was the only real one in the world.

Earlier this autumn
September is Oktoberfest looks at a festival that mostly happens in September. It began with a royal wedding in 1810, added an agricultural show the year after, and is known for a beer brewed in March and drunk in autumn. It is also where green hop beer, which Part One comes back to, is first mentioned.

Celebrity-Owned Pubs Worth the Pint (Not Just the Autograph)

Forget red carpets. In Britain, the celebrity trail is best followed with a pint glass in one hand and low expectations in the other.

Plenty of famous names have dipped into hospitality. The trick — and the point of this list — is telling the pubs a celebrity saved from the pubcos apart from the ones a celebrity built as a brand extension. The good ones are the pubs where the famous name is the least interesting thing about them.

Nine we can send you straight to. Three more we can't — because they aren't on Untappd, so PINtPOINT doesn't know them well enough yet to open the door.

London

The Grapes, LimehouseSir Ian McKellen · since 2011

Since 1583 (current bldg 1720s)Also: Sean Mathias, Evgeny Lebedev

The heavyweight of the list. Narrow Street, Limehouse, Thames views, low beams, dark wood. Charles Dickens namechecked it in Our Mutual Friend. Walter Raleigh reputedly launched from its steps. The current building went up in the 1720s on a site that had already been pouring for nearly a century and a half.

McKellen bought in with theatre director Sean Mathias and Evgeny Lebedev in 2011. Gandalf on the Thames is a fun sentence to say out loud, but The Grapes is a pub-first-and-famous-person-footnote-second — exactly how it should be.

Verdict Worth the detour. Go for the history, stay for the river. Best a pub can hope for from a celebrity name above the door: to be the least interesting thing on the wall.

The Fox & Pheasant, ChelseaJames Blunt (& Sofia Wellesley) · since 2017

Victorian pub since 1846Top 50 Gastropubs listed

Blunt and Wellesley bought The Fox & Pheasant after hearing their favourite local was at risk of closing. Which is a better origin story than most celebrity hospitality projects: not "brand extension", but "we actually drink here, so we bought it before someone flattened it".

Reopened 2018 as a polished-but-not-precious Chelsea/Fulham corner boozer — Victorian bones from 1846 kept intact, dartboard, wood-panelled fireplaces, a proper Sunday roast operation. Not likely to be your scruffy session pub. Very west London, and honest about it.

Verdict Strong Sunday lunch. More gastropub than beer-geek pilgrimage. Bonus points for the "we saved it" motive.

Lore of the Land, FitzroviaGuy Ritchie · since 2019

Own beer: Gritchie Brewing Co.British seasonal kitchen

Ritchie's pub interests are the closest thing on this list to a mini drinking empire — Lore of the Land is the central, accessible one. Three floors on Conway Street, near Warren Street tube. Serves his own Gritchie beer, brewed on his Ashcombe Estate in Wiltshire, and venison off the same farm.

Leans hard into the classic British pub ideal — warm lighting, proper food, curated drinks list, that slightly cinematic version of pub life where everything looks effortlessly old. Whether that reads as charming or a little too art-directed depends on your tolerance for authenticity-as-a-design-choice. Either way, the beer's genuinely his.

Verdict Good for a smart pint and food in town. Probably more polished than spontaneous. Get the Gritchie.

Bertie Blossoms, Notting HillEd Sheeran · since 2019

Full pub licence since 2020Portobello Road

A slightly stranger beast. Bought in September 2019 (reportedly for £1.5m) on the site of the long-loved Galicia Spanish restaurant. Tapas-leaning menu, three fireplaces, a dartboard. Got its full drinks licence in 2020, so it now works as a pub, not just a restaurant.

Reopened after refurbishment in March 2025. It's not the place you'd choose for a perfect pint of cask, but Portobello Road has always mixed drinking with tourism, and Bertie Blossoms is a coherent version of what that mix looks like from a global-pop-star angle. If you're already on the market, worth the loop.

Verdict More curiosity than classic. Include if the brief is "pubs and bars", not "pubs full stop". Better than it has any right to be.

Beyond the M25

The Farmer's Dog, Asthall, OxfordshireJeremy Clarkson · since 2024

British-produce-only ruleServes Hawkstone Lager & Cider

Bought for around £1m and reopened as The Farmer's Dog on 23 August 2024. Site was formerly The Windmill — on the A40 near Burford, Cotswolds. Famous for a British-produce-only rule that bans black pepper, avocado and Coca-Cola, and for the "Farmer's Puppy" marquee that swallows the overflow queue on Clarkson's Farm filming days.

Serves his own Hawkstone Lager and Cider (brewed in a rebuilt barn on Diddly Squat Farm). The kitchen leans hard into the on-brand thing. It is exactly what you'd expect and no more, which is either its strength or its problem depending on why you came.

Verdict Go for the Hawkstone, the "we only serve British" story, and the theatre. Best avoided on a filming day unless queueing is part of the fun.

The Royal Oak, Swallowcliffe, WiltshireJames May · since 2020

Grade II · dated 1705 on the chimneyCarries James Gin

May famously describes it as "buying half a pub". He's a part-owner, in with landlord Chris Byett and a group of village investors — a lockdown-era commitment when the pub trade was on its knees. The building is a Grade II 1705 tannery in a Wiltshire village on the Dorset border. Reopened in 2015 after seven years dark; May came aboard in 2020.

Country inn with rooms, straightforward kitchen, and his own James Gin range on the shelf. Absolutely a village pub, not a celebrity destination — the sort of place you might not clock the connection until someone said it out loud, which is the highest compliment a celebrity pub can be paid.

Verdict A proper village inn that happens to have a Top Gear presenter's name on the paperwork. Best for a real weekend, not a day trip.

The Half Moon, Kirdford, West SussexJodie Kidd · since 2017

Grade II · 15th-c. inglenookTop 50 Gastropubs listed

Model-turned-broadcaster Kidd bought The Half Moon in the summer of 2017. It's a Grade II listed 15th-century inglenook pub in a row of 1640 cottages, in a village near Billingshurst most people can't find on a map. Herb and veg garden feeds the kitchen; local Harvey's and Spitfire on the pumps.

Made the Top 50 Gastropubs list on merit, not on backstory. Genuinely one of the best country pubs in Sussex — the kind of place that would be worth the drive even if the owner was completely anonymous.

Verdict The one on this list most likely to deliver a perfect Sunday. Go hungry.

The Hand & Flowers, MarlowTom Kerridge · since 2005

World's first two-Michelin-star pub20 years in 2025

Kerridge is a stretch on the "celebrity" definition — he's a chef first — but with two decades on television and half a dozen books he now qualifies. What he built with Beth Cullen-Kerridge at The Hand & Flowers arguably invented the modern British gastropub template: first pub in the world to hold two Michelin stars, and still the only one to hold both plus the vibe of an actual pub you'd walk into for a pint of Rebellion.

Twenty years in in 2025. Marlow, West Street, no reservations for the bar side. The other side you book weeks out. Both sides are the same building.

Verdict If you're in Marlow, drop in for a pint on the bar side. If you're planning a weekend, book the dining side. Either way — a pub that owns two Michelin stars is still a pub.

The Tap & Run, Upper Broughton, NottsStuart Broad & Harry Gurney · since 2018

Ten real alesRebuilt 2023 after fire

The deep-cut on the list. Broad and Gurney's Cat & Wickets Pub Company took over The Tap & Run in 2018 after a two-year closure, ran it as a proper regional gastropub with a serious cask range, then had to rebuild it in 2023 after a tumble-dryer fire took the roof off. Reopened March 2023 as a "second innings". Ten real ales; cricket-themed without being obnoxious about it.

Vale of Belvoir, near Melton Mowbray. Cricketers running a proper Midlands country pub — no theatrics, no on-brand kitchen concept, just a good pub that got better after a fire tried to end it.

Verdict The one on this list closest to a "great pub that also has a celebrity link". Rebuild-story pubs punch above their weight; this one earns it.

Two more we couldn't send you to directly. Guy Ritchie's second pub, Lore of the Sky, sits on Compton Abbas Airfield in Dorset — smokehouse kitchen, open to walkers, drivers and light-aircraft pilots. Jade Thirlwall's Arbeia in South Shields (named for the Roman fort) is her deliberate hometown venture. Neither is currently on Untappd, so PINtPOINT doesn't index them yet. Log a beer at either through the app and we'll add them.

So, are celebrity pubs actually worth it?

Down the list: the best ones are the places where the famous name is the least interesting thing about them.

The Grapes would be worth the trip without McKellen. The Half Moon would be a Top 50 Gastropub whether or not Jodie Kidd owned it. The Hand & Flowers earned its Michelin stars before Kerridge became a TV name. The Royal Oak Swallowcliffe was a proper village inn before James May bought a bit of it, and would carry on being one if he sold up tomorrow. The Fox & Pheasant is a saved-from-closure story you can drink to.

At the other end: Bertie Blossoms and Lore of the Land are more obviously celebrity-era venues — good, but the backstory is part of the product in a way that would be dishonest to pretend otherwise.

Britain has always mixed drinking with theatre, gossip, literature, money and myth. Sometimes you follow the beer. Sometimes you follow the story. Best of all, you can do both — and PINtPOINT will help you tell one from the other.

FAQ

Which UK pubs are owned by celebrities?

The best-documented current examples (as of July 2026): The Grapes in Limehouse (Sir Ian McKellen), The Fox & Pheasant in Chelsea (James Blunt), Lore of the Land in Fitzrovia and Lore of the Sky in Dorset (Guy Ritchie), Bertie Blossoms in Notting Hill (Ed Sheeran), Porte Noire in King's Cross (Idris Elba — a wine bar), The Farmer's Dog in Oxfordshire (Jeremy Clarkson), The Royal Oak in Swallowcliffe (James May, part-owner), The Half Moon in Kirdford (Jodie Kidd), The Hand & Flowers in Marlow (Tom Kerridge), The Tap & Run in Upper Broughton (Stuart Broad and Harry Gurney), and Arbeia in South Shields (Jade Thirlwall).

What happened to David Beckham's pub in Notting Hill?

The Walmer Castle on Ledbury Road was bought by David Beckham and Guy Ritchie in 2018 and redeveloped as a Scottish whisky bar and restaurant. It was reported to have been taken over by hotelier Piers Adam in February 2022, so it's not currently a Beckham-Ritchie project despite still turning up in old listicles.

Are these actually good pubs, or just autographs?

Sort them yourself by the same test we did: would the pub be worth a visit if the celebrity owner had never touched it? The Grapes, The Half Moon Kirdford, The Hand & Flowers and The Royal Oak Swallowcliffe are yes-by-a-mile. The Fox & Pheasant and The Tap & Run are strong local pubs the celebrity involvement saved or rebuilt rather than invented. Bertie Blossoms, Porte Noire, Lore of the Land and Arbeia are more clearly celebrity-era venues — good, but the backstory is genuinely part of the product.

Does Jeremy Clarkson really only serve British produce at The Farmer's Dog?

Yes. It's the pub's founding rule: no black pepper, no avocado, no Coca-Cola. Everything on the menu has to be sourced from Britain, and the pub serves Clarkson's own Hawkstone Lager and Cider brewed at Diddly Squat Farm. Whether that's principled or gimmicky depends who you ask — but it's real.

What does it mean when James May says he "bought half a pub"?

Literally that. May is a part-owner of The Royal Oak in Swallowcliffe alongside landlord Chris Byett and a group of village investors — a lockdown-era investment when small country pubs were at their most vulnerable. He's not the sole proprietor, and he doesn't run day-to-day.

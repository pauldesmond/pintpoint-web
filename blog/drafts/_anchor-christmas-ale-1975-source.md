# Our Special Ale — notes for 1 December 2026

Angle: the Christmas beer that refused to say "Season's Greetings". Sourced
from David Burkhart, *The Anchor Brewing Story* (Ten Speed Press, 2022),
read 14 Sep 2026. Page refs where captured.

## The first one, 1975

- Brew **#151**, working title **"2nd L. A."** — a second Liberty Ale.
  Brew day **17 November 1975**.
- Explicitly not modelled on anything: "not anything at all like
  Ballantine's Burton Ale, Koch's Holiday Ale, or Noche Buena."
- All-malt, dry-hopped, "a novel, distinctly American twist" — **100%
  Cascade** in place of the Hallertau used in the first Liberty Ale.
- Fritz "bristled at the thought of calling this brew a holiday beer or
  wishing his customers 'Season's Greetings' or 'Happy Holidays'". Hence
  a plain **"Merry Christmas and a Happy New Year"** on the label. "The
  Ale wouldn't be watered down, and neither would its Christmas message."
- Vintage-dated like wine. Sold **only at the brewery**.
- Until **1987**: no printed neck label. Blank die-cut neck labels shipped
  loose so customers could write their own greeting and glue it on by hand.
- Linda: "That was so hand-done. And quick too, like **six hundred cases**?
  Loose-packed in 24-packs. And with the label that Fritz drew, the tree."
  Fritz sketched a simple tree on a vertical oval; **Jim Stitt** took it to
  the finish line.
- Label to the **ATF on 12 December**, approved in three days. First
  bottling **16 December 1975**, **6.5% ABV**.
- Slightly hazy — Burkhart attributes it to the Cascade dry-hopping.
  Siebel's eight-person panel: "robust and hoppy, with above average
  bitterness and afterbitter". Fritz's first **A+**.
- Maytag's own framing: because the first Liberty Ale had not been
  all-malt, this would be "the only all-malt and dry-hopped ale in the
  world, I think" — and, as far as he could discover, "the only real
  Christmas brew in the world", the tradition having lapsed even in
  Scandinavia.

## The recurring bit

- A fresh Stitt-illustrated label every year, **and a fresh recipe** — kept
  deliberately quiet. Fritz: "We did things that only a brewer would be
  interested in, but that a brewer would be very interested in. And we
  never mentioned it. We kept it a secret."
- UK afterlife: The Rake, Borough Market, stocking multiple vintages side
  by side in the late 2000s / early 2010s. An English-occasion beer made in
  San Francisco, shipped back, and cellared here across years.

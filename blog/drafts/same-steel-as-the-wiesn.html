PINtPRESSGuide19 November 2026 · London · Festivals

Same steel as the Wiesn. Different beer.

At noon on 19 September the new Lord Mayor of Munich drove a tap into the first barrel of the 191st Wiesn. About two months later Winter Wonderland opens: five steel loops, a £5 stein deposit and a Bavarian hall.

Hyde Park Winter Wonderland runs from 19 November 2026 to 3 January 2027. How much of it is actually Oktoberfest?

The five-loop coaster is the same one.

The rollercoaster

At Winter Wonderland the coaster is called Munich Looping. In Germany it is Olympia Looping, and the billing is the same at both ends of the trip: the ride’s own site calls it the world’s largest transportable coaster with five loops, and Winter Wonderland’s ride page calls it “the world’s largest transportable rollercoaster”. Wikipedia puts the whole structure at nine hundred tonnes.

It has been a fixture of Munich’s Oktoberfest since 1989, owned and operated by the Barth showmen family, and at Winter Wonderland since 2016, which the same history page gives as its first appearance anywhere in Britain. The design is Anton Schwarzkopf’s and Werner Stengel’s. Winter Wonderland markets it as “made famous at Oktoberfest”.

That is the part of Winter Wonderland that is Oktoberfest in the strongest sense: the Schausteller circuit. Olympia Looping’s own history page names the three events it appears at each year: Munich’s Oktoberfest, Hyde Park Winter Wonderland and the Wiener Prater. The Prater has since gone. Its own archive files the ride under former attractions, a guest for six seasons across 2016, 2018 and 2022 to 2025, the last of them for now, and building work on the Theater im Prater started on the plot in autumn 2025. The German text of that page is blunter: no open space is left in the Wurstelprater big enough for nine hundred tonnes of coaster. The same page names the two stops that remain, Oktoberfest yearly since 1989 and Hyde Park, and both are put up by the same showmen who build and tear down the Wiesn’s steel rides. The firm’s own imprint gives its registered address as Theresienhöhe 6, Munich.

How it got Bavarian

The Oktoberfest styling is not original to the park. Ride Rater, writing in December 2022, has the Royal Parks commissioning a small funfair in 2005 and 2006 that “generally passed without much attention”, and dates the change to 2007, when AEG Live and PWR were brought on board as organisers and, in its words, “it was the influence of Germany’s mighty Oktoberfest that began to transform Winter Wonderland into one of London’s biggest and most popular annual events”. The same piece has the event originally running from 1 December, with the mid-November start coming later.

The Bavarian Hall

Then there is the Bavarian Village, and inside it the Bavarian Hall. It is one address among many. Winter Wonderland’s site lists twenty-two places to eat and drink, and the Bavarian Village is one of them; the others run to Bar Ice, Bar Narnia, Thor’s Tipi Bar, the Princess Bar, Candy Cane Lane and an Après-Ski Party Resort.

This is the part that most obviously imports the shape of an Oktoberfest night: long tables, steins, hearty German food from Heidi’s Kitchen, and daily entertainment from Frontal and Luigi ‘The Machine’. It is family-friendly in the afternoon and tips toward adults in the evening. The operator’s own word for the chicken on its meal deal is “Oktoberfest-style”.

It is not a Wiesn tent. There is no official outpost status and no Maß of Festbier pulled under Munich rules.

Of the other corners, the operator’s own hire pages call Almhütte “our Oktoberfest-style beer hall” and Café Bavaria an alpine ski guesthouse over two storeys. One is the Wiesn, the other is the slopes. The park goes further outside the Village: over in the Arctic Circle, Winter Wonderland’s Après-Ski Party Funhouse promises moving steps, wobbly floors, a double slide and the snowy mountains of the Alps. The theme is adjacent to Oktoberfest without being the same thing. The form of it is pure Wiesn. The Oktoberfest’s own Jumanji is a funhouse on three levels of speedrolls, wobbly stairs and a glass labyrinth, which the city’s site recommends in the evening and after a visit to the festival tents, and its Crazy Island runs to five floors, fifty obstacles and a Caribbean theme. A walk-through that throws you about is a fairground staple at both ends. What gets painted on it is the showman’s business.

Several of those other addresses are worth the walk on their own terms. The Carousel Bar in Show Town is a working carousel with a bar on it, and the operator’s whole pitch for it is to spin slowly as you sip. That is a rarer thing to drink on than a long table.

The beer

Munich’s Wiesn will only pour beer from six houses: Augustiner, Hacker-Pschorr, Hofbräu, Löwenbräu, Paulaner and Spaten. The Historisches Lexikon Bayerns has the state-owned Hofbräu added in 1952 and the city concluding supply contracts with the other five since then, and says Munich allows only these breweries to supply the festival. Albert’s Schloss and Kapital Sheffield both advertised all six official Oktoberfest beers this autumn.

At the Bavarian Village the operator promises steins, bratwurst and a hot mulled wine, and offers a meal deal with a choice of an alcoholic or non-alcoholic drink. Winter Wonderland’s 2026 Bavarian Village page names no brewery, beer or strength; the Village’s own FAQ calls the draught Bavarian Gold. Its About page, updated in August, calls the Hall the place to get “steins of German beer”, and its home page for this season promises “authentic German beer directly from the keg”.

The stein is the tell. The Village’s FAQ says the draught is served in “our one-pint stein (568ml)”, against the Maß of a litre Munich pours, with a £5 deposit refunded when the stein comes back with its token. Other speciality beers and ciders can go in the same stein, the FAQ says, decanted from 500ml bottles.

Drinkers have been logging it on Untappd as Bavarian Gold, a 4.2% Helles carrying a one-line description: sold at Winter Wonderland, Hyde Park. Untappd files the Bavarian Village name as a contract brewery in Germany, and names Camerons Brewery of Hartlepool as the brewer.

The Hall’s own price board says otherwise. MyLondon photographed it there last season: Bavarian Gold, 4.2%, and directly under the name, “Brewed in Germany”. £4.10 the half, £7.95 the pint, £5 on the stein, with a Weiss and a Dunkel under the same name at £8.25 the 500ml and an Engel Kellerbier beside them.

The brand’s distributor puts it a third way. Ethnic Brand Marketing heads its Bavarian Gold page “An Authentic Taste of Germany” and then says the beer is “brewed under a UK License”. It gives the same 4.2%, a fifty-litre keg, and the Dunkel and Weiss that are on the board. The page names no brewery. It carries no date either, though that line was already on it in June.

So the brand is German on every record we have, and the brewing is not. Nobody publishes who brews it this season, the brand’s own site included, which bills it as a premium German beer at 4.2% and leaves it there. Neither is a Big Six Festbier, which is the only beer a Wiesn tent may pour, and the Untappd entry’s Winter Wonderland check-ins end last New Year’s Eve. This one has not opened, so it records what was poured, not what the taps will carry on 19 November.

What it costs

Getting in is cheap, and the box below has the detail: arrival slots from a pound, and a free entry ticket for anyone who spends £25 in advance. The rides are extra, and not trivially so. Winter Wonderland prices a standard ride on Munich Looping at £12 in advance. Eating is priced in the same register: a bratwurst meal deal from £12.50, the all-day Almhütte Experience from £42. The Village’s FAQ says a pint of Bavarian Gold cost £7.95 in 2025, and gives no price for this season.

Nine hundred tonnes of coaster come off the Theresienwiese and onto lorries for Hyde Park. Follow the beer’s Untappd entry and you end up in Hartlepool.

If you’re going

19 November 2026 to 3 January 2027, closed Christmas Day. Green Gate for the Village. The ticket is for Winter Wonderland itself rather than the Village: the Village’s FAQ puts some arrival slots at a pound and others at £5.50 or £8.25 in advance, and that ticket then covers Bavarian Village, the Hall included, though Winter Wonderland’s own page adds that it does not guarantee entry to Bavarian Hall or the other Village venues.

Winter Wonderland’s page says Bavarian Hall closes at 5pm from Wednesday to Sunday and reopens at 6:15pm to anyone without a table package; the Village’s own site says Thursday to Sunday. The evening session, 6pm to 10pm, is over-18s only, with passport or driving licence checked on a scanner at the door.

The all-day Almhütte Experience gives reserved seating with four drinks and a meal deal per guest; a soft-drinks version is offered too.

Winter is not Christmas

In 1971 Young’s gave an old beer a new name. Four years later Fritz Maytag visited the brewery while looking for something else.

In October 1975 Fritz Maytag walked round Young’s Ram Brewery in Wandsworth, went home to San Francisco and brewed a Christmas beer. As far as he could discover, it was “the only real Christmas brew in the world at that time”. Young’s brewed Winter Warmer. It does not catch him out, because the two are not the same beer.

Ram Brewery, 1971

Winter Warmer had not been called that for long. It was a Burton ale, the old strong dark English style that went out of fashion as drinkers moved to bitter and lager. In 1971 Young’s renamed theirs Winter Warmer.

A name like that tells a drinker when to want it. Whether the whole category of winter warmers dates from that decision is something we cannot prove. It would be a good story.

Harvest, then cold

Three things made midwinter the moment for the best beer of the year. The first was the grain: by late October the harvest was in. The second was the cold, because summer brewing spoiled before refrigeration.

The monasteries are the third reason. The story everybody tells is that Advent meant fasting, but liquidum non frangit ieiunium, liquid does not break the fast, so the brothers drank their dinner; and that the cellar ran three grades, the strongest for the abbot and the thinnest for the pilgrims at the gate. The Latin tag is real, but it is later than the monks who are always said to have relied on it. The three grades come from the Plan of St Gall, a ninth-century architectural drawing, and as the beer historian Martyn Cornell spent years pointing out, a plan is not a building.

Hospitality in a Benedictine house was a rule, chapter 53 requiring that every guest be received as Christ. What came out of the cellar for a midwinter guest is not recorded.

England’s own winter drink was the wassail bowl, which held strong spiced ale with sugar, nutmeg and ginger, and roasted crab apples dropped in hot until they burst, the pale froth giving the drink its name, lamb’s wool. On Twelfth Night it went out to the orchards, where drinkers poured ale over the apple roots and fired shotguns into the branches to frighten off whatever might spoil the crop. Christmas beer had a commercial life too: the beer writer Will Hawkes found the Phillips Brewery advertising its Christmas specialities in the Oxford Times in December 1888, and reckons Victorian Christmas beers often ran past 10%.

Leadenhall, 23 October

This year The Lamb Tavern, Young’s pub in Leadenhall Market, held its Winter Warmer Breakfast on Friday 23 October. Leadenhall Market calls it the Beaujolais nouveau of the beer world.

Young’s is not the only old English brewer still keeping a winter beer. Fuller’s pours Old Winter Ale at 5.3% and Shepherd Neame has its Christmas Ale on draught in December at 5.0%, and in bottle at 7.0%, while Greene King’s Fireside is a 4.5% ruby winter ale with, in their words, masses of grapefruit and lemon citrus characters.

Harvey’s of Lewes had been brewing Elizabethan Ale since 1953 when head brewer Anthony Jenner adapted it, in 1972, into something darker and dry-hopped for the season, and called the result liquid Christmas Pudding. When the brew is mashed, hops are laid around the mash tun and the beer is blessed. Miles Jenner’s favourite blessing was written by the Rev. Judith Egar.

The beer writer Phil Mellows, asked last December to pick ten Christmas beers anyone could find on a supermarket shelf, set a quick test: “Will it stir into a Christmas pudding?” Strength is not his objection. A cask ale a little over 4% “isn’t a problem as long as they’ve got the flavour”; the danger is “sticking a bit on tinsel on it and calling it Christmas”, and a festive tag on a beer no different from what you would drink all year is “just a marketing thing”. His typical example of the obvious answer is Harvey’s Christmas Ale.

Jeff Alworth, writing on Beervana on 23 October this year, went looking for the cold wet afternoon that makes a drinker want a porter and found the thermometer in the mid-70s Fahrenheit instead. He ordered the porter anyway.

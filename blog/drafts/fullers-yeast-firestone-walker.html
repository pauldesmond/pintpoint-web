The cleaner yeast Firestone Walker didn’t choose

Firestone Walker’s Double Barrel Ale took silver in the English-Style Pale Ale category at the Great American Beer Festival this month. The strain it was built on came out of Fuller’s in Chiswick, it won a tasting against the workhorse of American craft, and then it gave the brewery years of trouble.

Everyone knows the hops went west. Cascade, Centennial, Citra: the American aroma hop is the most retold story in modern beer, and the British end of it — Fuller’s and Timothy Taylor’s as the beers American brewers were trying to answer — gets retold with it.

The yeast gets told less often, and it is the part that stayed.

Why they chose it

David Walker, the English half of Firestone Walker and the W in its name, told us how the brewery picked the strain for Double Barrel Ale. What decided it was a tasting.

“The number given to the original DBA yeast (wyeast) we can’t recall at the moment, but it was a Fullers ESB yeast. Why did we use it? Because in beer trials, that’s the one we liked best even though the Chico yeast was cleaner.”

That last clause is the whole thing. Chico — the Sierra Nevada strain, the workhorse of American craft — was the cleaner ferment and they knew it. They took the London one anyway, because the beer was better.

What it cost them

A yeast that drops bright is a gift to a brewery reusing it. It is also a yeast that leaves early.

“The Fullers yeast was a prodigious producer and flocculator, which was beneficial when reharvesting yeast for future brews. But that same flocculation characteristic lead to Diacetyl issues.”

Diacetyl is the buttery, butterscotch note that most brewers spend their time getting rid of, and the way they get rid of it is to leave the yeast in the beer long enough to take it back up. A strain that flocculates hard has already sunk to the bottom of the tank by then. The clean-up crew clocks off early.

Walker describes what that meant in a young brewery’s laboratory:

“In the early days our lab, such as it was, could measure diacetyl in the tank and its reduction during maturation, but we couldn’t measure its precursor. Our issues with diacetyl occurred during packaging when the hidden precursor mixed with a little oxygen and turned into diacetyl.”

That is the trap, and it is a precise one. They could watch the fault fall away in the tank and sign the beer off. What they could not see was the compound waiting behind it, which needed only a little oxygen at packaging to become the thing they thought they had already cleared.

“It was a great yeast when we could tame it, but a nightmare otherwise and we adjusted within a few years.”

Fuller’s in America

A Fuller’s strain was in an American yeast catalogue to be chosen at all because Fuller’s had become a name American brewers knew. Jeffrey House founded Thames America Trading Company at the turn of 1980 and brought London Pride and ESB to the United States, ESB on draught from 1987, and we have written before about the other direction of that trade — Anchor’s beers crossing back in Fuller’s returnable kegs for a London pub trial in 1991.

That is the background, not the mechanism. Walker’s strain came from Wyeast, a laboratory in Oregon, and nothing in his account ties the choice to House’s imports. What the imports explain is why a Californian brewery in the 1990s would think of Fuller’s at all.

What survived

Within a few years, then, the strain was changed. But read what it was changed into:

“We eventually morphed to a derivation of this and more reliable for our needs; now our OG House Ale yeast. Still essentially a Fullers/London yeast.”

Not replaced — derived from. Walker does not say how that derivation happened, and we have not asked him to reconstruct it: whether it was selected out of the original culture over successive generations, or adapted, or arrived at some other way, is not in the record. What is in the record is his description of the result. The house culture of one of California’s most decorated breweries is, in the words of the man who co-founded it, still essentially a London yeast — Firestone Walker has been brewing since 1996 and Double Barrel Ale was the first beer they made; how old today’s culture is in its own right, as against the line it descends from, he does not say.

One honest gap, since it is the only one that matters here. The original Wyeast strain number has not been recalled, so the paper trail stops at the name. This is one brewery, one beer and one account, from one of the two people who built it, given to us with permission to quote.

On the morning Walker wrote this, Double Barrel Ale took silver in English-Style Pale Ale at the Great American Beer Festival.

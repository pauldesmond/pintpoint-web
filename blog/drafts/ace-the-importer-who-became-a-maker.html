A container load of cider he hadn’t made yet

Jeffrey House spent ten years selling other people’s cider in America. Then he made his own, and called it ACE.

Jeffrey House did not go looking for cider. He was trying to sell London Pride, one Irish pub at a time, along Geary Street in San Francisco.

Ireland’s 32 said no. So did the Abbey Tavern, and Pat O’Shea’s Mad Hatter. At the last pub on the street, Kieran Burns turned him down too, then asked him, in rather stronger words, where his cider was.

Burns told him to go after Bulmers of Hereford or Taunton of Somerset. It was the early eighties, and Thames America had a new drink to sell. House dates his own time in cider from 1983.

Thames America’s cider years

Bulmers already had an American importer for Strongbow and Woodpecker. So in 1983 House went to Taunton.

There were two Blackthorns to choose from. Thames America picked Taunton’s medium Special Vat and sold it as Blackthorn, judging the Dry too dry for American drinkers. A trade report of the late nineties counted the Taunton ciders as the first imported draught ciders sold in the United States.

Bars had no room for another cooler, so Taunton supplied about fifty glycol chillers. Blackthorn went on one spigot and London Pride on the other. In time there were around a hundred taps, and two to three hundred 50-litre kegs a month.

“We made Blackthorn the number one draft cider in the Western States.”

The business moved to the fourth floor of 211 Sutter Street, the old KSAN radio offices, with a warehouse at Pier 23 on the Embarcadero.

Ten years, then his own

House has a rule about importing, and he has seen it work from both ends.

“When you import brands, you usually have about 10 years to market them. The best thing to do is do a moderate job because if you do a bad job, they fire you. If you do a very good job, they get rid of you because they think somebody else can do better because you’re a small company.”

Fuller’s went to Grolsch Imports in 1990. Blackthorn went to Miller.

“Having lost a couple of brands, Fuller’s to Grolsch, Blackthorn to Miller, I thought, well, the next one I lose, if I’m going to lose one, I’m going to own it.”

The California Cider Company

He started it in 1993.

“I started the California Cider Company up in Sonoma in 1993, and we contract made Ace Cider from all the local apples in Sebastopol, California.”

House told a trade writer in the nineties that he settled on ACE after realising “jack” was already in use.

ACE Apple came first.

The Lucky order

In 1993 House and David Cordtz got an appointment with Don Brown, the new beverage buyer at Lucky’s, Safeway’s biggest rival.

“If you’re lucky, you get ten minutes or so with a chain buyer.”

House offered the same Blackthorn deal he had with Safeway. Brown wasn’t interested. He asked instead whether they had a local Californian apple cider in 22-ounce bombers, at $24 a case. They hadn’t bottled any ACE yet.

“But I lied and said yes.”

Brown wanted a container load: 1,200 cases. The first bottling run came in 1994, and that is how ACE went into bottles. A trade report later named Brown as the buyer who approved the first bottled ACE for Lucky stores in Northern California.

By 1997 the company was selling more than 70,000 case equivalents a year, and a pear cider had joined the apple in six-packs.

House makes a bigger claim for those early years.

“We were the first cidery in the Western United States after Prohibition.”

A building in Sebastopol

The business had already found an office. In 1989 House had bought 714 Penny Royal Lane for Thames America, and it became California Cider’s future home.

The cidery needed somewhere bigger. Warren Dutton’s family, local apple and wine growers, built a building for it, gave him three years rent-free, and took 5% of the company in return.

In early 1998 the trade press reported an ACE cider pub in the planning, possibly the first in the country. It opened in Sebastopol in 1999, as the Ace in the Hole.

“We had the first cider pub in America, the very first in 1999.”

Pineapple

All three of his sons came into the business as it grew. ACE kept adding fruit. In 2013 it launched a pineapple cider, which House calls the world’s first. It became, he says, the number one fruit cider in the United States.

In House’s account the cidery grew into the biggest cider-making plant in the western United States: twenty-four stainless fermenters of eight thousand gallons each, capacity for five million gallons a year, and 1.3 million case equivalents going out of it. The trade counts a brand as significant, he says, once it sells a million case equivalents.

ACE reached every state, he says, New Hampshire last of all, and was the first craft cidery to do it. It was named an Impact Hot Brand year after year.

In The Cider King he puts the growth in one line: revenue went from $3 million in 2010 to $25 million in 2021.

The sale, and going back

On 16 November 2021 he sold the California Cider Company, Thames America and the ACE brands to Vintage Wine Estates for $47 million plus earnouts. He had turned down their first offer. What restarted it was running into their man at the deli counter of his local supermarket and asking him, standing there, whether he still wanted to buy the company. He said yes.

Vintage Wine Estates went into bankruptcy in 2024, and the brand went to two sisters, Reva Lipton and Gila Lipton Blum. House offered to come back and run it, and they agreed.

He went back as general manager for about eight months. In his telling, the job was getting the business “back up from zero, back up to 50 percent”, and looking after what he had built.

What it became

According to ACE’s own history, it is still made in Sebastopol, at the original cidery, from local apples. The name on the tap is the one he chose in 1993.

House started in cider by being asked for it in an Irish pub. He ended up making it, and by his account selling it in all fifty states.

“We actually not only built a brand, we helped build a category that had been lost in America.”

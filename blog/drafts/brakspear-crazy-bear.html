Brakspear’s £3.35m Crazy Bear deal. Two hotels, two very different futures.

On 27 October administrators were appointed to two country hotels, one in Oxfordshire and one in Buckinghamshire, and both closed the same day. Two days later the freeholds belonged to a pub company from Henley-on-Thames that has not brewed a pint since 2002.

Neither building reopens as the thing it was. One of them is not going to be run by its new owner at all.

What shut

The Crazy Bear was founded by Jason Hunt at Stadhampton in 1993 and was known, as The Caterer puts it, for its eclectic room design. The original had twenty-three boutique bedrooms, two restaurants, a bar, a terrace and events space for up to two hundred and fifty. The Beaconsfield hotel opened in 2008 with thirty-five bedrooms, two restaurants, a bar, an outdoor swimming pool and room for a hundred and fifty.

Untappd files the Stadhampton site under three categories at once: hotel, Thai restaurant and gastropub. That is a fair description of what the Crazy Bear was trying to be, and of why it was difficult to replace.

The timing was cruel at Beaconsfield. The hotel had launched a new restaurant, Fable, under head chef Pete Gray one week before the administrators arrived. That launch itself came eight months after the closure of Bear by Carlo Scotto, a fourteen-cover chef’s table that lasted three months.

The money, and what it is not

This was the second administration in under two years. The business went in during December 2024 and came out in January 2025, bought back in a pre-pack by its own directors, Richard Booth and Tom Etridge, for £6.5m. Companies House shows Etridge resigning as a director of the two parent companies, CB Opportunities 3 and CB Opportunities 4, that September.

On 27 October 2026 Kiri Holland and Danny Dartnaill of BDO were appointed joint administrators to those same two companies. Confirming the sale two days later, Holland said the business had been “impacted by challenging market conditions and a quieter than expected summer trading period”, and that while the sale had secured the freeholds, “unfortunately it has not been possible to preserve the hotel operations”.

About a hundred and thirty people worked at the two hotels. Most were made redundant, with a small number kept on to help the administrators. Every event booking, room, spa and restaurant reservation was cancelled.

Brakspear paid £2.5m for Beaconsfield and £850,000 for Stadhampton, which is where the £3.35m comes from. Set that against the £6.5m of twenty-two months earlier and the drop looks brutal, but the two prices are not measuring the same object. The first bought a business that was trading. The second bought two closed buildings with the bookings already cancelled and the staff already gone.

The buyer

Brakspear brewed in Henley-on-Thames until October 2002 and has been a pub company ever since. Chief executive Tom Davies told the Morning Advertiser this week that the group runs about a hundred and twenty-five sites. Turnover for the year to 28 December 2025 was £47.9m, up sixteen per cent, with the managed Honeycomb Houses division reporting twenty-three per cent sales growth.

That division has been doing the buying. The Grade II listed Stroud Hotel, twenty-four bedrooms with a functions and dining business, reopened under Honeycomb Houses on 10 November with its existing team in place, taking the division to thirteen sites. Earlier in the year three Surrey pubs came from Grosvenor Pubs & Inns: the Cricketers on the Green, the Kings Arms and the Duke of Cambridge.

Two futures

Beaconsfield is the one Brakspear intends to run. It joins Honeycomb Houses after what Davies calls a substantial redevelopment, as “a classic Honeycomb House, with quality bedrooms and a quality food and drink offer”. The swimming pool that came with it has given him an idea: “We think we have the potential for a bit of a spa offer there.” Work is expected to start in the second half of 2027 and run about four months, which puts the doors opening in early 2028, subject to planning.

That date has already moved once. On 5 November the Morning Advertiser reported the reopening as targeted for the second half of 2027. A fortnight later the second half of 2027 is when the builders go in.

Stadhampton is the one he is minded to let. Discussions are under way with events operators, and Davies is explicit about why: “I potentially feel that it could be a better owner-operated events business than a classic managed house model.” Running it directly has not been ruled out, and he hopes to have it open towards the end of the first quarter of 2027.

So the cheaper building is meant to be open by the spring, under somebody else’s management, doing roughly what it already did. The dearer one stays dark for more than a year and comes back carrying the company’s own name. The reputation for weddings and parties, which is the thing that survived at Stadhampton, is precisely the reason it is being handed to a tenant rather than kept.

What a pub company sells now

The same interview has Brakspear installing two padel courts at the Frogmill in Gloucestershire, with planning applications in for two more sites. The courts have been running a few weeks and Davies expects to understand the demand by the spring.

His reasoning is worth quoting in full, because it is the argument behind the whole deal. “We want to have as many different income streams as possible because just opening and servicing food and drink is limiting.” And, on where the growth comes from: “It’s not all about acquisition for us. It’s also about maximising the opportunities that we have in our existing estate.”

On buying, he is waiting for sellers to catch up. “Profitability is down in hospitality venues and that needs to be reflected in the price.” Another tenanted pub is in the pipeline. The Chequers in Marlow closes in January for a major refurbishment.

The shape of it

A brewery that stopped brewing has bought a hotel group that stopped trading. What it wants from the deal is two freeholds in its own corner of the country: Stadhampton is ten miles from the brewery site at Henley, and Beaconsfield fifteen the other way. The beer with Brakspear on the label is made under contract in Surrey. The estate is the business now, and an estate is buildings.

Which is why the two hotels get different answers. A big eccentric building with a wedding trade is worth owning and worth letting. A big eccentric building with a swimming pool is worth owning and worth running. The Crazy Bear tried to be both at once, in both places, and the administrators’ line is the epitaph: the buildings could be saved and the operation could not.

Brakspear say they now run a hundred and twenty-five pubs. Forty of them have a page here, grouped by area from Henley outwards. Neither of these two is among them, and on this timetable neither will be for a while.

The dates

27 October 2026. Kiri Holland and Danny Dartnaill of BDO appointed joint administrators to CB Opportunities 3 and CB Opportunities 4. Both hotels close. All bookings cancelled.

29 October 2026. Sale of the freeholds to Brakspear confirmed. Of about a hundred and thirty staff, the majority are made redundant.

End of the first quarter of 2027. Stadhampton, bought for £850,000, hoped to reopen, probably leased and tenanted as an events business.

Second half of 2027. Work starts at Beaconsfield, bought for £2.5m, subject to planning. About four months of it.

Early 2028. Beaconsfield reopens as a Honeycomb House, possibly with a spa.

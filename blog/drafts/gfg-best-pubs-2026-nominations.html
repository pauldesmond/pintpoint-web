The 12 pubs we're backing for Good Food Guide 2026

Nominations for The Good Food Guide's Best Pubs 2026 close at 11:59pm on Thursday 13 August. Twelve PINtPOINT favourites, from a Bloomsbury Victorian to a Sheffield station bar.

A pub earns a place on this shortlist by being worth going out of your way for. Not "the best in postcode X" — the ones with a specific reason. The Good Food Guide's Best Pubs 2026 nominations use four criteria — uniqueness, deliciousness, warmth, strength of recommendation — and we've picked twelve that answer all four. Half London, half the rest of the country, none of them anywhere by accident.

London

The Anchor & Hope

Waterloo, SE1

The blueprint for the modern London gastropub — no bookings, chalkboard menu, one sitting-a-night flavour of purposefulness. Cask well-kept on a rotating handful of pumps; food from a kitchen that has trained a generation of British chefs to think about the pub as a serious room. Twenty-something years in and it hasn't drifted.

The Cadogan Arms

Chelsea, SW3

A proper King's Road pub with a proper King's Road kitchen. Refurbished by JKS Restaurants in 2021, done with restraint — the dining room upstairs is a serious operation, the ground-floor bar is still where you'd go for a pint. Beer taken seriously; food taken very seriously.

The Lamb

Bloomsbury, WC1

Victorian snob screens on the bar — the swivelling etched-glass panels that let the barmaid see out but the punters not see in. One of the last complete sets in London. The pub around them is a Young's-run Grade II, cask kept properly, and food that doesn't have to be clever because the room isn't.

The Spaniards Inn

Hampstead, NW3

Sixteenth-century tollhouse on the edge of Hampstead Heath. Byron and Dickens both drank here; both left it standing. A proper garden, proper roast, and a fire in the corner from October to April. The kind of pub Americans think England is full of, and mostly isn't.

The Bull & Last

Highgate Rd, NW5

The destination pub at the top of Highgate Road — Kentish Town if you're walking there, Hampstead Heath if you're walking away. Kitchen consistently rated one of London's best gastropubs; menu leans big and seasonal; cask ale kept for people who actually drink it. Book upstairs, walk in downstairs.

The George Inn

Southwark, SE1

The last galleried coaching inn in London, owned by the National Trust, run as a working pub. Cask ale in the courtyard on a summer evening is, no exaggeration, one of London's civic rituals. The food is capable and correct. Dickens borrowed it for Little Dorrit.

Ye Grapes

Mayfair, W1

A pub in a Mayfair mews that is somehow neither a tourist trap nor a lawyer canteen. Sunday roast is what you go for; the rest of the week you go for the taxidermy and the fact that Shepherd Market is one of the most improbable pockets of central London that still functions as a village.

Regions

The Marble Arch

Manchester

The most beautiful pub interior in Manchester, and it isn't even close. Green mosaic tiled walls up to the ceiling, sloping barrelled floor, Marble Beers brewed a hundred yards up the road. Food is above the room's pay grade — Manchester pub food at its confident best.

Sheffield Tap

Sheffield

Inside the Grade II Edwardian first-class refreshment room at Sheffield station. Thornbridge on cask, Tapped brewed downstairs, and a curry menu that gets the correct proportion of station-bar hurry and Sheffield seriousness. Miss your train on purpose.

The Bell

Ingatestone, Essex

A proper Essex village pub with a proper Essex village kitchen. Adnams, Doom Bar, a decent pinot noir. The bar is separate from the dining room and both feel like they'd miss you if you didn't come in this week.

Radio City Social

Chelmsford, Essex

Twelve-tap taproom in Chelmsford's old radio-city arches — Marconi's ghosts on one side, twelve keg + cask lines on the other. Kitchen runs pizza and hand-rolled hot dogs to a standard that punches well above the room's grade. Best small brewery-adjacent pub in Essex.

The Ale House

Chelmsford, Essex

Chelmsford's cask room of record. Six or seven hand pumps, no fuss, and a kitchen that does the things pub kitchens should. If you drink cask in Essex regularly, you know it; if you don't, this is the room to start in.

Curation vs the algorithm

The Good Food Guide picks pubs the way a knowledgeable friend does — with taste, prejudice, and a memory. PINtPOINT picks pubs the way a network of data can — with what's on the taps tonight, what's rare, what's near you.

The two jobs are different. One tells you where to go on a specific evening; the other tells you where to plan a Saturday around. They meet in the middle — a curated shortlist is only as useful as the freshness of the tap list under it; a live tap list only means something if a human's already told you the pub is worth the trip.

All twelve above are in PINtPOINT, many with live tap lists. If GFG's judges want to know what any of them are pouring the day they visit, we're the app for that. In the meantime, please back them: nominations close 13 August and the wider the shortlist, the better the eventual list.

UK Oktoberfest Events 2026

At noon today the new Lord Mayor of Munich, Dominik Krause, needed two blows of the hammer to drive the tap into the first barrel, and shouted “O’zapft is!” Two is a good score. He had said beforehand he hoped for two or three, and it was his first go at it.

The 191st Wiesn runs until 4 October. If watching it has given you ideas, here is where Britain is pouring — sorted by the only question that matters once the oompah starts: is the beer in the stein actually from Munich?

You can watch the whole thing from here. Bavaria’s own broadcaster, BR24, streams the tapping and the rest of the fortnight on YouTube — the parade of the landlords, the Trachten procession on the first Sunday, and the tents themselves. It is in German, and it does not matter in the slightest.

Why that question is worth asking

Only six breweries may pour at the Wiesn — Augustiner, Hacker-Pschorr, Hofbräu, Löwenbräu, Paulaner and Spaten — and what they pour is Festbier: pale, dry, and built to be drunk by the litre for sixteen days. That is the beer being tapped in the tents this afternoon, and it is not the beer the festival started with.

Most British Oktoberfest nights are a German lager and an oompah playlist, which is a perfectly good night out and nobody should be sniffy about it. But a handful put genuine Wiesn beer in the stein, and if you have just watched Krause swing the hammer, you probably want to know which is which. So this list is in two halves, and the first half is short.

Where the actual Wiesn beer is

Go here if you care what is in the glass. One rule for reading this section: “all six” is the venue’s claim, not our count. Both places making it advertise the Big Six without printing a full list, and neither names Augustiner — the one brewery of the six that barely leaves Bavaria. That is an absence from a webpage, not from a bar: it may be poured, or bottled, or neither. Worth asking before you build an evening around it.

19 Sept – 25 Oct

Albert’s Schloss

London (Soho) · Manchester · Birmingham · Liverpool All six claimed

The UK gold standard, and all four cities run the same season — we checked. Free entry, keg tapping, 19 September to 25 October at each. One caveat: their page bills “all of the Munich Big 6”, then names five — Spaten, Paulaner, Löwenbräu, Hacker-Pschorr, Hofbräu. Augustiner is not among them, which would figure: it is the hardest of the six to get out of Munich. It may be there in bottle. Ask.

30 September · 7–10:30pm

Caps and Taps

Tufnell Park, London 3 of 6 — Paulaner, Augustiner, Hofbräu

Which answers the question the paragraph above leaves open. A party keg tapped at seven with hammer and tap, and the £15 ticket is a stein’s worth — two pints — of Paulaner Oktoberfest and a pretzel. Behind it, in bottle: Augustiner, with Weihenstephan, Hofbräu and Flotzinger, plus Sierra Nevada Festbier on keg. This is the only place on this list that names Augustiner — the one of the six that barely leaves Bavaria, and the one both all-six venues decline to print. It took a bottle shop. Sold out, and worth knowing about for next year: they run it every September.

18 Sept – 11 Oct

Kapital

Sheffield All six claimed

The strongest Big Six claim outside Albert’s, and the reason to go north for it: all six Festbiers in 1L steins, with a Paulaner wooden-barrel tapping on Fridays. The site says “all six official Oktoberfest beers” without listing them, and Paulaner is the only one it names.

Radio City Social

Chelmsford 3 of 6 — Löwenbräu, Hofbräu, Spaten

The only entry here we did not have to take anyone’s word for. Radio City Social publish a live tap list, and on 22 September it carried Löwenbräu Oktoberfestbier at 6.1%, Hofbräu at 6.3% and Spaten at 5.9% — the official strengths, to the decimal, sitting on a guest list in an Essex bar with no tent, no ticket and no oompah. Newbarns’ Festival Bier at 5.6% and Sierra Nevada’s at 6% are on alongside them. In the fridge rather than on the bar, and worth asking for: Giesinger Festbier at 6% — a Munich brewery brewing to Wiesn strength that the tents will not have, the whole argument of this page in one bottle — plus Ayinger Fest-Märzen at 5.8% from just outside the city, and two Augustiners. One catch, and it is the point of this page: the fourth German name on the bar is Hacker-Pschorr Oktoberfest Märzen at 5.8%, the export Märzen, not the 6% Festbier they brew for the Wiesn — and those Augustiners are the Lagerbier Hell at 5.2% and the Weissbier at 5.4%, the everyday lager and the wheat, not the 6.3% Oktoberfest Bier nobody here seems able to get. So five of the six names are in the building and three of the six beers are. The one missing altogether is Paulaner, which is the easiest to find anywhere else on this page.

Radio City Social

19 Sept – 31 Oct

Bermondsey Bierkeller

London Bridge Paulaner

Long-running cellar on Tooley Street with Paulaner on tap — one of the six, done properly as a Bierkeller night out, and six weeks of it from the grand opening on 19 September. The week has a shape: oompah band Thursdays and Saturdays, the Hosen Stage Band on Fridays, a bottomless Bavarian brunch on Saturday afternoons, and fire and stilt acts on Saturday nights. Two packages — the Feast at £45–55 gets a reserved bench, two steins and a food token; the Party at £20–30 is entry and a bench.

Oktoberfest on the Common

Clapham Common Paulaner partner

UK’s big outdoor tent, and by their own account Paulaner’s only official UK Oktoberfest partner — a named Wiesn brewery on the bar at party scale. They pour two south London breweries beside it, Jubel of Lambeth and Sambrook’s of Wandsworth, which no other big tent on this list can say.

3 October · 6–11pm

Fulham Pier

Fulham, SW6 Löwenbräu

Held back from the first version of this list until its dates firmed up; they have. One night, five hours, in the Orange Box on the lower ground floor. Löwenbräu is one of the six — an actual Wiesn brewery rather than a German lager standing in for one, which is worth more than a fortnight of most things on this page. Three tickets: £15 to get in, £25 with a stein of it, £40 with a stein and a sausage. Band, DJs, roaming performers, and the Thames outside.

The Rake

Borough Market, SE1 Hofbräu

No event, no oompah, no lederhosen — just Hofbräu Oktoberfestbier at 6.3% on the list at Borough Market, where it was still showing on 22 September. One of the six, poured without ceremony next to a Doppelbock, a Budvar and a couple of dozen other things, which is a very Rake way to mark the season — they call themselves London’s original beer bar and have never needed a theme. If you want the Wiesn beer and not the party, this is the shortest route to it in central London.

19 September · 6pm

Tiny Rebel Brewery Bar

Rogerstone, Newport 2 of 6 — Hacker-Pschorr, Paulaner

One night, and the most interesting bar on this list for anyone who read the companion essay: Hacker-Pschorr Oktoberfest Märzen at 5.8% alongside Paulaner Oktoberfest Bier — the old amber beer and the modern pale one, on the same bar, on the day Munich tapped. Guest beers, food specials and Oompah Cymru, which is a better band name than anything Bavaria has managed.

19 September – 4 October

Brewhemia

Edinburgh 2 of 6 — Paulaner, Hacker-Pschorr

Scotland’s entry, and it prints its taps, which is more than most: Paulaner and Hacker-Pschorr Oktoberfest brews, plus Flotzinger Wies’n-Märzen and Bitburger Premium Pils, in steins, with bottled German beer behind. Two of the six named outright and a third Bavarian that only Caps and Taps otherwise carries. Sixteen days on Market Street rather than a weekend, and the brass is booked by name across the three weekends — Flat Cap Brass on the 19th, The Brass Rats on the 25th and the 2nd, Atomic Brass on the 26th, Loud Noises on the 3rd. Not to be confused with Edinburgh Oktoberfest at Central Hall in the list below, which is a different event a week later.

Brewhemia Oktoberfest

In the spirit

Same energy, looser about the exact Munich six. Steins, sausages, brass, and a crowd. Dressing up is a bonus rather than the point. Thirty-eight of them, by region, so you can find the one near you and stop reading. Then one at the end that is not an event at all.

Wherever you are

For everyone the three lists above do not reach — no tent within travelling distance, or a weekend already spoken for. One chain has put a freshly brewed Festbier inside thirteen towns, several of which have no tent at all. Failing that, the beer will come to you.

19 Sept – 4 OctBrewhouse & Kitchen
Thirteen brewpubs, Chester down to Worthing — the only entry on this page that runs Munich’s exact dates, 19 September to 4 October, opening and closing on the same days as the Wiesn. Every site brews on the premises, so this is not one beer thirteen times: the 2026 pair is a Festbier at 5.6% and a Helles at 4.8%, and Untappd already has Chelmsford’s Helles at exactly that strength and Portsmouth’s older house Oktoberfest as a 5.5% Märzen. And the Festbier is not just a promise on a poster: a 5.6% Oktoberfest Märzen was checked in at their Cardiff brewpub on 20 September — somebody drinking the thing, at the strength advertised, the day after it started. Same badge over the door, different beer in the glass, which is the whole argument for a brewpub chain. Bratwurst, currywurst, pork knuckle and a pretzel-and-pint deal alongside; Dorchester runs an oompah party on Saturday 3 October. Cardiff, Cheltenham, Chelmsford, Chester, Bournemouth, Dorchester, Gloucester Quays, Highbury, Lichfield, Portsmouth, Southampton, Southsea and Worthing.
Any night · 0.0 milesHome Sweet Home
Your sofa — The closest venue on this page, and the only one that is not one of the fifty-seven. All six of Munich’s will post to you, which is more than anywhere above this line can say. They sit close together — Hofbräu and Augustiner at 6.3%, Löwenbräu 6.1%, Paulaner 6%, Spaten 5.9%, Hacker-Pschorr 6% — because a Wiesn beer has to: 13.5% Stammwürze minimum, which lands at about six. Beer Merchants carry four of them, roughly £3.65–4.30 a bottle. Beers of Europe have the lot in stock: Löwenbräu, Hofbräu, Spaten and Paulaner at £3.69, Hacker-Pschorr at £3.79, and Augustiner — the one no venue on this page pours, which Caps and Taps could only get in bottle before selling out — at £4.39. Which is the quiet joke of the whole list: the six are harder to drink in a tent than to buy in a box. British brewers worth the postage instead: Anspach & Hobday — already on this list, for Croydon — Lost & Grounded, Newbarns, Braybrooke, Donzoko and Pilot all have a Festbier out this autumn, and Left Field, who we wrote about in April, add Cloudwater’s, Verdant’s and Polly’s. No ticket, no bench, no oompah. Dress up anyway.

One more, and it is not Oktoberfest. Alexandra Palace bills “London’s largest German Bier Festival” for Saturday 7 November — but it sits inside the Fireworks & Drone Festival, gates at four, from £11.50, with a Queen tribute band and a drone show rather than an oompah tent. Two days after Bonfire Night, a month after Munich packed up. Worth saying because several London round-ups put it on 30–31 October: the palace’s own page says 7–8 November, and the bier hall is the Saturday. We have not counted it among the fifty-seven, because it is not one of them — it is here as the last place in the calendar that will hand you a German beer in a field.

Know one we have missed?

This list is fifty-seven events, and it is certainly not all of them. Eight of them came in from readers within days of publishing — Tiny Rebel, Flat Iron Square, Anspach & Hobday, The Old Brewery, The Dial, The Destination at Lakeside, Caps and Taps and Triple Point Brewing — which is exactly how this is supposed to work. Local beer halls, one-night stein parties, a brewery tap doing a Bavarian weekend — if you know one that should be here, tell us and we will add it: hello@pintpoint.co.uk. If you know which beers it actually pours, even better.

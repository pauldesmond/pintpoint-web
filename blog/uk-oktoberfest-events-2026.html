UK Oktoberfest Events 2026

At noon today the new Lord Mayor of Munich, Dominik Krause, needed two blows of the hammer to drive the tap into the first barrel, and shouted “O’zapft is!” Two is a good score. He had said beforehand he hoped for two or three, and it was his first go at it.

The 191st Wiesn runs until 4 October. If watching it has given you ideas, here is where Britain is pouring — sorted by the only question that matters once the oompah starts: is the beer in the stein actually from Munich?

You can watch the whole thing from here. Bavaria’s own broadcaster, BR24, streams the tapping and the rest of the fortnight on YouTube — the parade of the landlords, the Trachten procession on the first Sunday, and the tents themselves. It is in German, and it does not matter in the slightest.

Why that question is worth asking

Only six breweries may pour at the Wiesn — Augustiner, Hacker-Pschorr, Hofbräu, Löwenbräu, Paulaner and Spaten — and what they pour is Festbier: pale, dry, and built to be drunk by the litre for sixteen days. That is the beer being tapped in the tents this afternoon, and it is not the beer the festival started with.

Most British Oktoberfest nights are a German lager and an oompah playlist, which is a perfectly good night out and nobody should be sniffy about it. But a handful put genuine Wiesn beer in the stein, and if you have just watched Krause swing the hammer, you probably want to know which is which. So this list is in two halves, and the first half is short.

Where the actual Wiesn beer is

Thirteen venues pour at least one of the six. Go here if you care what is in the glass. One rule for reading this section: “all six” is the venue’s claim, not our count. Both places making it advertise the Big Six without printing a full list, and neither names Augustiner — the one brewery of the six that barely leaves Bavaria. That is an absence from a webpage, not from a bar: it may be poured, or bottled, or neither. Worth asking before you build an evening around it.

19 Sept – 25 Oct

Albert’s Schloss

London (Soho) · Manchester · Birmingham · Liverpool All six claimed

The UK gold standard, and all four cities run the same season. Free entry, keg tapping, 19 September to 25 October at each. One caveat: their page bills “all of the Munich Big 6”, then names five — Spaten, Paulaner, Löwenbräu, Hacker-Pschorr, Hofbräu. Augustiner is not among them, which would figure: it is the hardest of the six to get out of Munich. It may be there in bottle. Ask.

Caps and Taps

Tufnell Park, London 3 of 6 — Paulaner, Augustiner, Hofbräu

Which answers the question the paragraph above leaves open. A party keg tapped at seven with hammer and tap, and the £15 ticket is a stein’s worth — two pints — of Paulaner Oktoberfest and a pretzel. Behind it, in bottle: Augustiner, with Weihenstephan, Hofbräu and Flotzinger, plus Sierra Nevada Festbier on keg. This is the only place on this list that names Augustiner — the one of the six that barely leaves Bavaria, and the one both all-six venues decline to print. It took a bottle shop. Sold out, and worth knowing about for next year: they run it every September.

18 Sept – 11 Oct

Kapital

Sheffield All six claimed

The strongest Big Six claim outside Albert’s, and the reason to go north for it: all six Festbiers in 1L steins, with a Paulaner wooden-barrel tapping on Fridays. The site says “all six official Oktoberfest beers” without listing them, and Paulaner is the only one it names.

25–26 Sept · three sessions

Oktoberfest at Kirkstall Brewery

Leeds 4 of 6 — Hofbräu, Löwenbräu, Paulaner, Spaten

They publish the whole beer list, all twenty of it, which almost nobody does — and four of them are Munich’s: Hofbräu Oktoberfestbier 6.3%, Löwenbräu 6.1%, Paulaner 6% and Spaten 5.9%, every strength matching the official figure to the decimal. That is the highest count on this page anyone has actually shown their working for. The fifth German name is Hacker-Pschorr Oktoberfest Märzen at 5.8% — the export Märzen, not the 6% they brew for the Wiesn — so four, and a fifth that nearly counts. Augustiner is the gap, as it is everywhere. Past the six it stays serious: Weihenstephaner Festbier, Hefeweissbier and Helles, Tegernseer Hell, three Erdingers, two Fürstenbergs, Veltins, a Pülleken Helles, and four of Kirkstall’s own German-style beers including a Klosterkeller Keller Pils and an Altstadt Altbier. Friday 6–11pm, Saturday noon–5 and 6–11, with Bratwurst Brass playing two sets a session and karaoke after half nine on the evening ones. Tickets from £5.50, over-18s, not dog-friendly. Last year sold out.

Radio City Social

Chelmsford 3 of 6 — Löwenbräu, Hofbräu, Spaten

Radio City Social publish a live tap list, and on 22 September it carried Löwenbräu Oktoberfestbier at 6.1%, Hofbräu at 6.3% and Spaten at 5.9% — the official strengths, to the decimal, sitting on a guest list in an Essex bar with no tent, no ticket and no oompah. Newbarns’ Festival Bier at 5.6% and Sierra Nevada’s at 6% are on alongside them. In the fridge rather than on the bar, and worth asking for: Giesinger Festbier at 6% — a Munich brewery brewing to Wiesn strength that the tents will not have, the whole argument of this page in one bottle — plus Ayinger Fest-Märzen at 5.8% from just outside the city, and two Augustiners. One catch, and it is the point of this page: the fourth German name on the bar is Hacker-Pschorr Oktoberfest Märzen at 5.8%, the export Märzen, not the 6% Festbier they brew for the Wiesn — and those Augustiners are the Lagerbier Hell at 5.2% and the Weissbier at 5.4%, the everyday lager and the wheat, not the 6.3% Oktoberfest Bier nobody here seems able to get. So five of the six names are in the building and three of the six beers are. The one missing altogether is Paulaner, which is the easiest to find anywhere else on this page.

19 Sept – 31 Oct

Bermondsey Bierkeller

London Bridge Paulaner

Long-running cellar on Tooley Street with Paulaner on tap — one of the six, done properly as a Bierkeller night out, and six weeks of it from the grand opening on 19 September. The week has a shape: oompah band Thursdays and Saturdays, the Hosen Stage Band on Fridays, a bottomless Bavarian brunch on Saturday afternoons, and fire and stilt acts on Saturday nights. Two packages — the Feast at £45–55 gets a reserved bench, two steins and a food token; the Party at £20–30 is entry and a bench.

The Rake

Borough Market, SE1 Hofbräu

No event, no oompah, no lederhosen — just Hofbräu Oktoberfestbier at 6.3% on the list at Borough Market, where it was still showing on 22 September. One of the six, poured without ceremony next to a Doppelbock, a Budvar and a couple of dozen other things, which is a very Rake way to mark the season — they call themselves London’s original beer bar and have never needed a theme. If you want the Wiesn beer and not the party, this is the shortest route to it in central London.

19 September · 6pm

Tiny Rebel Brewery Bar

Rogerstone, Newport 2 of 6 — Hacker-Pschorr, Paulaner

One night, and the most interesting bar on this list for anyone who read the companion essay: Hacker-Pschorr Oktoberfest Märzen at 5.8% alongside Paulaner Oktoberfest Bier — the old amber beer and the modern pale one, on the same bar, on the day Munich tapped. Guest beers, food specials and Oompah Cymru, which is a better band name than anything Bavaria has managed.

19 September – 4 October

Brewhemia

Edinburgh 5 of 6 on draught — all six in bottle

Scotland’s entry, and the deepest Big Six line-up on this page: five of Munich’s six on draught — Hacker-Pschorr, Hofbräu, Löwenbräu, Paulaner and Spaten — and all six in bottle, which makes it the only venue here that names all six and says which format each one comes in. Albert’s Schloss and Kapital claim all six too; neither prints a list. The sixth is Augustiner, in bottle because, as their own account of it puts it, Augustiner is the only one of the six that still pours exclusively from wooden barrels. Alongside them Flotzinger Wies’n-Märzen, a Bavarian that only Caps and Taps otherwise carries, and Bitburger Premium Pils, in steins. Sixteen days on Market Street rather than a weekend, and the brass is booked by name across the three weekends — Flat Cap Brass on the 19th, Atomic Brass on the 26th, Loud Noises on the 3rd — a Leeds brass band whose record is twenty-three pop and dance covers, Bad Romance to Smooth Criminal, and not one oompah number among them. The Brass Rats have cancelled the 25th and the 2nd. Not to be confused with Edinburgh Oktoberfest at Central Hall in the list below, which is a different event a week later.

Brewhemia Oktoberfest

In the spirit

Same energy, looser about the exact Munich six. Steins, sausages, brass, and a crowd. Dressing up is a bonus rather than the point. Thirty-eight of them, by region, so you can find the one near you and stop reading. Then one at the end that is not an event at all.

Wherever you are

No tent within travelling distance, or the weekend already spoken for? One chain has put a freshly brewed Festbier inside thirteen towns, several of which have no tent at all. Failing that, the beer will come to you.

Questions people actually ask

Which UK Oktoberfest events actually serve real Munich beer?

Sixteen of the seventy-six entries on this page pour at least one of Munich’s six licensed Wiesn breweries. Albert’s Schloss and Kapital claim all six. Kraft Werks in Nottingham and Brewhemia in Edinburgh pour five, everything but Augustiner — and Brewhemia has the sixth in bottle, so every Wiesn brewery is drinkable in one room. Kirkstall Brewery in Leeds pours four and publishes the full list. Caps and Taps, Munich Cricket Club and Radio City Social in Chelmsford pour three. Tiny Rebel Brewery Bar pours two. Bermondsey Bierkeller, Brigadiers, Oktoberfest on the Common, The Cockpit in Chislehurst and Aberdeen Oktoberfest at CHEERZ pour Paulaner, Fulham Pier Löwenbräu, and The Rake at Borough Market has Hofbräu on the list with no event attached. Everything else on this page is a German lager and an oompah band, which is a perfectly good night and a different thing.

What are the six official Oktoberfest beers?

Only six Munich breweries may pour at the Wiesn, and the list has been closed since the nineteenth century: Augustiner and Hofbräu at 6.3%, Löwenbräu 6.1%, Paulaner and Hacker-Pschorr 6%, Spaten 5.9%. They cluster there because a Wiesn beer must be brewed within the city to a minimum 13.5% Stammwürze, which lands at about six per cent.

Is Hacker-Pschorr Oktoberfest Märzen the beer served at Oktoberfest?

No, and this is the most common substitution in Britain. Hacker-Pschorr brew two: Oktoberfest Märzen at 5.8%, the amber export beer sold here, and Oktoberfestbier / Superior Festbier at 6%, the pale one actually served in their Wiesn tent. Three venues on this page pour the 5.8% Märzen. It is a good beer; it is not the Wiesn beer. British importers stock the Märzen because the Märzen is the one that gets exported.

Which UK venue pours the most Munich beer?

Kirkstall Brewery in Leeds, at four of six — Hofbräu 6.3%, Löwenbräu 6.1%, Paulaner 6% and Spaten 5.9%, every strength matching the official figure. It is also the only one that publishes its whole twenty-beer list, so it is the highest count anyone has shown their working for. Two venues claim all six and publish no list.

Why is Augustiner so hard to find in the UK?

Augustiner is the smallest and least exported of the six, and the only one no venue on this page pours on draught. Caps and Taps in Tufnell Park managed it in bottle and sold out. Their everyday Lagerbier Hell is a different matter: seven UK pubs now pour it on draft, after years in which nobody here could get it at all. But that is the 5.2% everyday lager, not the 6.3% Oktoberfest Bier brewed for the Wiesn — the same right-brewery-wrong-beer swap that catches people out with Hacker-Pschorr. For the Oktoberfest one, mail order is the realistic route.

Is Oktoberfest Essex the same as Oktoberfest Guildford?

Yes — both are stops on one nine-city tour, along with Chichester, Colchester, Milton Keynes, Southend, Beverley, Plymouth and Portsmouth. Each town gets its own domain and its own local-sounding name, and none of the nine sites mentions the others. They share one ticketing account. It is a real event either way; it is not a local institution.

When is Oktoberfest 2026, and when do the UK events run?

Munich’s 191st Wiesn ran 19 September to 4 October 2026. British events run far longer — from 11 September to 31 October, with one German bier hall at Alexandra Palace on 7 November. Two entries match Munich’s exact window: Brewhouse & Kitchen straight through across thirteen brewpubs, and Clacton Pier across its three weekends.

Are any UK Oktoberfest events free?

Several. Clacton Pier is free entry across all three weekends, Triple Point Brewing in Sheffield is free on both nights, and The Destination @ Lakeside charges nothing to get in. The Dial in North Greenwich needs no ticket at all. Elsewhere tickets start at £5 — Anspach & Hobday in Croydon, BrewDog Waterloo and Oktoberfest Southampton all open there, and Kirkstall is £5.50.

Know one we have missed?

This list is seventy-six events, and it is certainly not all of them. Eight of them came in from readers within days of publishing — Tiny Rebel, Flat Iron Square, Anspach & Hobday, The Old Brewery, The Dial, The Destination at Lakeside, Caps and Taps and Triple Point Brewing — which is exactly how this is supposed to work. Local beer halls, one-night stein parties, a brewery tap doing a Bavarian weekend — if you know one that should be here, tell us and we will add it: hello@pintpoint.co.uk. If you know which beers it actually pours, even better.

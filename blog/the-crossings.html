The Crossings

Five essays on the beer traffic between Britain and America, and the Londoner who moved a good deal of it.

Beer crossed the Atlantic in both directions and the same name keeps appearing on the manifest. Jeffrey House sold Fuller’s ESB to America, watched American brewers take more from it than a good pint, lost the agency in 1990, and started making cider in Sonoma instead. In between, a London yeast went west and stayed, and Anchor’s beers came east into three London pubs. Gordon A. Ponce named the shape of it, the Transatlantic Beer Bridge, writing at The Craft Beer Finder on House’s research.

These are the spans we have written, and they are not in the order they were published. Two go west, one stays, and two come back.

One · The beer goes west

Before he could sell ESB to America, he had to sell it to Fuller’s

The first pour was at the College Inn in Seattle on 3 November 1987, and the argument that got it there was with the brewery, not the market. Fuller’s head brewer did not want ESB bottled, so America got it on draught. Then the brewers who drank it started borrowing from it.

Two · The yeast goes west

Why Firestone Walker chose Fuller’s yeast

A London strain went into a Californian fermenting tank and stayed there. What it does to a beer brewed a continent and an ocean from Chiswick, why they wanted it, and what has crossed since.

Three · The importer stays

A Cider of His Own

An importer gets about ten years with somebody else’s brand. It happened to House twice, and the second time he stopped importing and built ACE in Sonoma, out of a pack of cards, a building the Duttons put up for him and an order he could not yet fill.

Four · And the beer comes east

Anchor Brewing’s British heritage runs deeper than people realise

Fritz Maytag came to England in 1975 and went home with two beers in mind. Liberty Ale reached a British handpump on 4 July 1992, at the White Horse at Parsons Green, and Michael Jackson announced it that morning.

Five · And the hops come back

The Return Crossing

What British brewers made of American hops once they could get them. The road runs through Kelham Island’s Pale Rider, Thornbridge’s Jaipur and BrewDog’s Punk, and it starts at the White Horse, where the span above ends.

With thanks to Jeffrey House, whose recollections, papers and corrections run through all five essays.

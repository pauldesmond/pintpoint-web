The Whippet at Liverpool Street

The Whippet opened just outside Liverpool Street Station on 14 May 2026, the first new site under that name since the Holborn Whippet closed in December 2020.

If you are here for the German lagers: we have since sorted seventy-six UK Oktoberfest events by whether the beer in the stein is actually from Munich. Sixteen pour at least one of the six. Most do not. →

This is the new venue from Bloomsbury Leisure Group, the team behind the Euston Tap. The soft launch ran on 14 May 2026 from around 4pm, with the full opening shortly after. The group already runs specialist rooms across London, Manchester and Bristol.

The name carries a local history. Bloomsbury and Holborn parishes had a 19th-century habit of following an afternoon of whippet racing with ale and a plate of coal-grilled meats. The original Holborn site stitched that into its identity: cask from small breweries, a coal grill, a basement room for food and games. The pool, food and entertainment at EC2 follow the same pattern.

The closure wasn't voluntary either. The original traded for about eight and a half years on Sicilian Avenue before folding in December 2020, displaced when the Holborn Links Estate changed hands. In late 2024 the group teased a four-times-larger "Whippet 2.0" for High Holborn. It didn't happen. EC2 is the plan that landed.

Augustiner was the teaser.

Early-June boards showed Bitburger Pils, Pivo Kutna Hora Czech Pils, Tegernseer Hell, Benediktiner Hell and Sapporo on keg, alongside Boddingtons on cask. The Munich headline never landed; the lager list is where the interest turned out to be.

The opening line-up

The operator's own framing, from a general-manager job ad posted before the opening: "a brand-new pub … built around one simple idea: exceptionally well-kept cask ale and perfectly poured pints." The role spec lists "proven cellar management experience (essential)" and "strong cask ale knowledge" ahead of the usual hospitality boilerplate.

The group named its opening brewery line-up on the @WhippetEC2 account: Green Duck, Bristol Beer Factory, Joseph Holt, Floc, Otter, Ossett and Boddingtons. The phrase they used was that these are breweries "generally locked out of the big chains in London".

By early June the keg list included Bitburger Pils, Pivo Kutna Hora Czech Pils, Tegernseer Hell, Benediktiner Hell and Sapporo. Beamish is on draft too, the Cork stout that has been competing with Guinness since 1792; sister site The Bolter has poured it since February. The food is gyoza, where the original Holborn site had coal-grilled meats.

Another station-side beer bar

Their formula is station-pub specialist beer: Euston, Waterloo, Farringdon and Aldgate in London, Piccadilly, Victoria and Oxford Road in Manchester: Taps outside major stations in two cities. The Whippet directly outside Liverpool Street is the first time they've run that play under the Whippet name rather than the Tap name.

The formula has heritage. The original Euston Tap opened on Bonfire Night 2010 as a joint venture between Bloomsbury Leisure and Jamie Hawksworth of Pivovar, the Yorkshire group that already ran Sheffield Tap and Pivní York and effectively invented the rail-adjacent Northern Tap concept. The two groups co-owned Euston Tap for years; Bloomsbury runs it solo today and built the rest of the Tap family from the playbook the partnership left them.

The lager list and Boddingtons are doing different jobs here

The famous Munich badge is not on the bar. The lager run is Bitburger Pils, Pivo Kutna Hora, Sapporo, Tegernseer Hell, Benediktiner. Five of the ten keg lines: a German pils, a Czech pils, two Bavarian Hells and a Japanese rice lager.

The launch-week list, three weeks earlier, ran Bitburger, Sapporo, Tegernseer, Bohemia Regent, and Moretti, five of ten keg lines, all continental. Two have rotated out since. Bohemia Regent → Pivo Kutna Hora: a Czech-pils-for-Czech-pils swap, but Kutna Hora is the more highly regarded of the two. Moretti → Benediktiner Hell swaps an Italian lager for a Bavarian monastery brewery's Hell.

And the lagers arrive in their own branded glassware. Benediktiner Hell in the Benediktiner glass with the monk badge. Tegernseer Hell in the HTB crown-and-cross. Sapporo in the star glass. Three brewery glasses, stocked and rotated for three lagers on the same bar.

Sparkler-on cask service is a Northern-English signature, almost never poured in London, a small piece of plastic on the end of a hand-pump that has been splitting British cask drinkers for 140 years. The cask itself, the venue's tap list confirms, is brewed on contract by J.W. Lees in Middleton, a family brewer, rather than at AB InBev scale. The second handpull is pouring Timothy Taylor's Boltmaker, CAMRA's Champion Beer of Britain in 2014, alongside it. The brewery's four-time winner is Landlord.

The June keg list also carries Deya Steady Rolling Man, The Kernel Pale Ale, and Howling Hops NEIPA alongside the lager run.

Last pour, first pour

Untappd keeps the RSS archive for closed venues, which means the final pint poured at the original Holborn Whippet is still on record. It was a Cold Spark by Big Smoke Brew, logged at 19:13 on Friday 11 December 2020. Eight days before London was ordered into Tier 4.

The bar said on 13 May that the Norman Cornish prints that hung in the original Holborn room are now going up on the EC2 walls, a few weeks after opening. Cornish was the Durham painter of working men's pub and street scenes, the mood the original Whippet quietly built its room around. Different walls, same pictures.

The Whippet opened from around 4pm on Thursday 14 May, standing room only that first night, with the fixed seating arriving the Friday. Five and a half years after that final pull, there is a first pour again under the same name, a short walk from where the last one happened.

Sit down if you can. The beer is the same either way; the pint reads better from a chair.

Frequently asked questions

Where is The Whippet?

The Whippet is a new bar from Bloomsbury Leisure Group, located outside Liverpool Street Station in the City of London (postcode EC2). It is the same team behind the Euston, Waterloo, Farringdon and Aldgate Taps in London, the Piccadilly, Victoria and Oxford Road Taps in Manchester, The Crofter's Rights in Bristol, and Bloomsbury Bowling Lanes.

When does The Whippet open?

The Whippet soft-launched on Thursday 14 May 2026 from around 4pm and has been open since.

Who runs The Whippet?

Bloomsbury Leisure Group, one of the UK's most active specialist beer operators. Their Taps run in two cities: Euston, Waterloo, Farringdon and Aldgate in London; Piccadilly, Victoria and Oxford Road in Manchester. They also hold The Crofter's Rights in Bristol, which is not a Tap. Plus the Pelt Trader, The Bolter and Jackalope in London, and bowling alleys with beer on both ends: Bloomsbury Bowling Lanes in London, The Lanes in Bristol. The Whippet is their latest London opening.

Is The Whippet the same as the Holborn Whippet?

No. Different building, different area, different room. But the name carries deliberately. The original Holborn Whippet, run by the same group, closed in 2020 and built a loyal following. EC2 is not a reopening, but it is the first new site under the Whippet name since Holborn closed in December 2020.

What will be on tap at The Whippet?

June 2026 update: The Whippet still is not pouring Augustiner. What it is pouring, though, is one of the more unusual lager selections for a craft-leaning City bar: Bitburger Pils, Pivo Kutna Hora Czech Pils, Tegernseer Hell, Benediktiner Hell and Sapporo, plus Boddingtons on cask through a sparkler. Only one of those Hells is brewed in Bavaria: Benediktiner is made under licence for the Ettal monastery at Lich in Hesse. The bigger story turned out not to be one famous Munich name, but a quietly serious continental lager mix.

What is a sparkler and why does it matter on cask Boddingtons?

A sparkler is a small nozzle fitted to the end of a cask hand-pull. The pressure comes from the pull itself; the sparkler restricts the flow so the beer is agitated on its way out, which produces a tight, creamy head with different texture and mouthfeel to an unsparkled pour. It is the standard cask-service style across Northern England; London has traditionally pulled without one.
